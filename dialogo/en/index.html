How it started

I live in Madeira and my Portuguese is not good enough yet. In the first five days I attended five online seminars in Portuguese: I asked questions about my own work, the speakers answered, and the ideas from each session now sit as cards on my shelf. This is how the pairing of “a person and their AI pilots” works.

Three levels of taking part

1. Shelf

works

The seminar is recorded, transcribed and digested. Five ideas in your own words and a line on “what I applied” sit as a card on your “Learning” shelf. A month later you remember not “some webinar” but exactly what you took into your work.

2. A voice in the session

tested live

One or two questions on the substance of the topic and of your own work, in the seminar's language. The speaker answers a question that matters to the whole group, and that is useful to organisers and participants alike.

3. Prompter

being built

The translation follows the speaker line by line, and the question takes shape while they are still on the topic. The aim: to react as fast as native speakers and to know the speaker and what they say on a par with them.

How the speed changed

~30 minutesThe first sessions. Recording in twenty-minute parts, transcription, translation, question. By then the speaker had moved on to another topic.
minutesAt the fifth session the organiser turned on captions and the speed went up: the pilot reads them off the screen and translates at once. If there are no captions, you can ask for them in advance.
secondsThe next step. The prompter: line-by-line translation and a question at the press of a button. We are building it and measuring on live sessions.

Six stages of one seminar

1. The day before
  • Registration confirmed, the organiser's email found, the event in the calendar.
  • A one-page brief on the speaker: role, company, recent posts, website, books.
  • Two or three questions in the seminar's language, with translation. Each tied to an agenda item and to your work.
  • A glossary of the topic: 15–30 terms that are sure to come up.
  • If the session has no captions, you can ask the organiser for them in advance.
2. Recording
  • The recording runs for the whole session and stays in a personal archive. The shelf is built from it afterwards.
  • Ten seconds after the start, a check: it is recording and the file is growing.
  • You get a message either way: recording or not recording.
3. Live understanding
  • The session's captions and their translation as the talk goes on.
  • A snapshot of every new slide and its text.
  • The seminar chat: what matters is translated straight away.
  • A short summary in your language: what is being discussed and what concerns you.
4. Taking part
  • We listen through the first half.
  • From the middle, one or two questions on what was said, from the practical side of your work.
  • Only what you said “yes” to goes into the chat.
  • After the speaker's answer, one line: thanks and the takeaway.
  • At the end, a thank-you to the speaker, the moderator and the organiser in one message.
5. People
  • Who runs the series, who speaks, what is asked in the chat.
  • Whose questions and topics are close to your work: these are people worth meeting in person.
  • We write to people one at a time and for a reason. No mailings to the participant list.
  • Speakers in one series know each other. We speak to each in our own words.
6. Shelf and contact
  • A digest in your language: 10–12 thoughts and what we take into work.
  • A card on the shelf: five ideas in your own words, your question, the speaker's answer and “what I am applying”.
  • The speaker's LinkedIn: a follow and a comment under their post about the seminar.
  • The organiser's feedback form: honest ratings and one specific detail of the session.
  • If the speaker stays silent, we do not push.

Who does what

AI pilots

  • Speaker brief and questions
  • Recording and checking it
  • Translation and summaries
  • Chat translation
  • Digest and draft card

The person

  • Joining the session and access codes
  • A “yes” to every text
  • Sending to the chat and to LinkedIn
  • Their own words in the card
  • Deciding whom to meet

Rules we do not break

Honesty and other people's data
  • The recording and the transcript stay in a personal archive. The shelf holds only your own thoughts and a link to the source.
  • We ask the organiser for captions openly and in advance.
  • A cloud recorder is visible to all participants. We use it only with the organiser's knowledge.
  • Translation can be wrong, so only text checked by a person goes into the chat.
